Night Work

Utaona Dust

Lilpoet Ezra

The road does not argue. It waits under the shoe, under the matatu smoke, under the late call you almost did not answer. By morning, dust has learned your name. It gathers at the hem, at the ankle, inside the small silence between what you wanted and what Nairobi allowed. Still, you walk. Not loudly. Not as proof. You walk because the body has its own old faith, because home is sometimes only a direction, because the night can take plenty and still leave you with one more breath. Utaona dust, they said. And you did. You saw it rise behind every leaving, soft as memory, patient as grief, gold in the headlights for one brief second before darkness closed its hand.
