Midnight on a Nairobi Rooftop

Lilpoet Ezra

At midnight, Nairobi keeps its face half-lit. The rooftops hold their breath. Below, engines pass like tired prayers, and I stand where the wind can find me, watching the city turn loneliness into light. Your name does not arrive. Only smoke from an old hope lifting slowly from the chest, thin as a prayer that already knows the sky will not answer. The hours lengthen. Memory moves through me without hurry. I learn again how silence can bruise without making a sound. Someone has lost a phone. I wipe my face before she sees it, borrow a calm voice, and ask, "Ulipoteza wapi?" as if I am not also searching. We find what she lost. Her relief returns clean and immediate. Mine stays somewhere above the street, caught between the moon, the rooftop, and the thing I cannot recover. The city keeps moving. That is its mercy. That is also its cruelty.
